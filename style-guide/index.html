01 / BRAND FOUNDATIONS

Clear. Confident.
Human.

Let the event lead. Use the brand to make it recognizable, with enough space for the information to breathe.

01

Lead with one message

One headline, one supporting idea, one next step. Put longer context in the caption.

02

Keep the originals

Use approved photos and original logo files. Preserve colors, proportions, and the complete mark.

03

Adapt the conversation

Keep the same event facts across channels. Change the hook, length, and call to action for the audience.

The logo is the signature.

Use assets/logo.svg as the full brand mark. Reserve at least one quarter of its displayed height as clear space on every side. This is a proposed project rule, not an official corporate measurement.

  • Keep its original aspect ratio and colors.
  • Place it on a clean white logo shelf, with other uploaded partner logos.
  • Use the full logo at least 100 px wide in a 1080 px visual as a starting point; inspect legibility after upload.
  • Use contain-fit: no cropping, stretching, recoloring, effects, or AI redrawing.
APP ICON / FAVICON

The icon helps people navigate.

Use assets/favicon.svg for browser tabs and compact interface identity. Keep the original geometry and colors. Give it a plain background and a little breathing room.

  • Use the full logo for social content branding.
  • The favicon does not replace the required logo shelf.
  • Keep the icon separate from copy and partner logos.
  • For uploaded MVP logos, export transparent PNG or use a high-quality JPG/WebP. The current upload control accepts these raster formats.

A starting ratio for designed areas, excluding photographs and original logos. Use additional colors sparingly; all eight do not need to appear in one visual.

Color needs a readable partner.

Use at least 4.5:1 contrast for regular text and 3:1 for large text. White text on the main cyan is below both thresholds. Ink on cyan and white on red work for regular text.

04 / WORDS & LANGUAGES

Sound like people.
Stay precise.

Use an energetic, welcoming, practical voice. Invite people to do something specific; keep event facts exact.

Instagram

Start with a benefit or a question. Keep paragraphs short. Use a few relevant hashtags in feed copy. Story copy is a quick prompt to act.

TRY

Bring an idea. Leave with a working prototype.

Facebook

Give the community a reason to care. Include who the event is for, what happens, date, place, and a clear next step. Prefer complete, readable sentences.

TRY

Join fellow students for an evening of building and sharing.

Google Chat

Make it easy to scan in a conversation. Start with the event name, then the essential details and link. An optional *bold* title is enough; skip hashtag blocks.

TRY

Creative Automation Lab — 21 November. Event details and registration: [event link].

EN + UA

Two languages. Two complete versions.

Select English, Ukrainian, or both before generation. Create a separate visual and matching caption for each chosen language. Keep each visual in one language; use the supplied translations of names and proper nouns.

  • Use language code uk for Ukrainian and en for English. “UA” is a display label.
  • Keep the same dates, venue, URLs, speaker names and facts across versions.
  • Localize month names and the wording of the CTA. Include the event time zone when a time is given.
  • Check Cyrillic glyphs, apostrophes, line breaks and text fit in the exported image.
  • Use one clear CTA. Preserve the real destination; invent no link, speaker, price or registration deadline.

TYPE / WORKING DEFAULT

Arial. Clear in both scripts.

Use Arial with a sans-serif fallback for consistent browser/Canvas output. Headlines: bold, line height around 1.1–1.2. Supporting copy: regular or medium, around 1.4–1.6. Use sentence case and avoid tight letter spacing in Ukrainian.

05 / ASSET LOGIC

Every logo.
Each photo with intention.

Style and generation rules work together. Keep the identity consistent without repeating the same photograph throughout a feed.

ALWAYS

All logos on every visual

Place all uploaded original logos on the white shelf. Align them optically at comparable visual weight. Keep each logo’s proportions, edges, and internal details visible. Give partner marks equal breathing room.

If there are many logos, use rows. If they become illegible, revise the composition before publishing rather than removing a partner mark.

ONCE PER NETWORK / RUN

One photo, one appearance

Each uploaded photo appears at most once within a social network, across formats, variants, and languages. Instagram feed and story share one photo pool. A photo can be used again for Facebook or Google Chat.

After the photo pool is exhausted, switch to information-only compositions with the event details and every logo. A new generation run starts a new allocation.

A small example

2 photos + Instagram post and story + 2 languages + 2 variants = 8 outputs. Use photos in exactly 2 outputs; make the other 6 information-only. Facebook gets its own photo allocation.

06 / READY TO HAND OVER

A useful pack.
A careful final check.

Export PNG visuals and UTF-8 captions. The ZIP manifest connects every image to its format, language, variant, input and photo assignment.

  1. Check the facts. Title, date, time zone, venue, language and CTA agree with the brief.
  2. Check the identity. Every original logo is complete and readable. No photo repeats within the network.
  3. Check the view. Open the full-resolution PNG and the destination app’s mobile preview. Inspect contrast, crops, safe areas and Cyrillic text.
  4. Check the pack. Every delivered PNG has a matching caption. Partial failures are recorded in the manifest.
Using this guide with the MVP

Studio implements original-logo compositing, per-network photo allocation, language selection, channel-specific copy and ZIP export. Finalization applies the shared safe-area margins and original logo shelf. AI-generated lettering needs manual review; minimum raster type sizes cannot be measured reliably. Final destination-app previews and publishing remain part of human review.

The shared JSON policy and per-channel directions are included in separate planner, image-generator and copywriter prompts. Planner and copywriter run in parallel; the image API receives the plan, assigned photo and logos. Review each visual, request revisions through its feedback field, and approve every visual and post before ZIP export.
